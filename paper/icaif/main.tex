\begin{abstract}
Diffusion, score-based, and flow-matching models have rapidly entered
quantitative finance as generators for prices, returns, limit order books,
financial tables, and structured objects such as volatility surfaces and
risk-neutral paths. This survey organizes the emerging literature by the data
object modeled by the generator. We review the core modeling families, summarize
representative finance applications, and highlight the evaluation problem that
currently limits comparison across studies: financial generators must satisfy
not only generic distributional fidelity, but also tail behavior, temporal
dependence, market-microstructure realism, privacy, and economic constraints.
The result is a compact taxonomy and research agenda for diffusion-family
models in finance.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{diffusion models, score-based generative models, flow matching, finance, survey}

\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Why diffusion, why now.}
Generative modeling is useful in finance because many important regimes are rare,
private, or expensive to observe: market crashes, stressed liquidity, credit
defaults, customer-level fraud, and realistic high-frequency order flow.
Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)~\cite{ho2020ddpm} and
score-based generative models~\cite{song2019ncsn,song2021sde} offer stable
training, strong mode coverage, and flexible conditioning. Those properties make
them attractive for synthetic financial data, scenario generation, forecasting,
and decision support, where GAN-style instability and mode collapse are
especially costly.

\textbf{Contributions.}
We make three contributions. First, we introduce a data-first taxonomy that
organizes diffusion-family finance papers by the object being modeled:
financial time series, limit order books (LOBs), tabular records, and other
structured financial objects. Second, we connect those data domains to common
applications--generation, prediction and inference, trading and portfolio
construction, risk and stress testing, and privacy. Third, we summarize open
problems around benchmarks, tail fidelity, market validity, privacy, and
non-stationarity.

\textbf{Scope and taxonomy of this survey.}
We include learned diffusion, score-based, flow-matching, rectified-flow, and
stochastic-interpolant models when they are applied to financial data or provide
directly relevant backbones. We exclude classical financial ``diffusion''
models in the purely SDE/PDE sense unless they contain a learned generative
sampler. Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomy} shows the organizing principle: the data
object is the primary axis; application and method family are secondary axes.
This differs from generic time-series diffusion surveys~\cite{yang2024tssurvey},
tabular-diffusion surveys~\cite{challagundla2025tabsurvey}, and broad
synthetic-data surveys~\cite{li2025gensynthsurvey}, because finance imposes
domain-specific requirements: no-arbitrage and measure consistency, heavy tails,
stylized facts, microstructure realism, and privacy/governance.

\section{Background}\label{sec:bg}
This section gives the minimum machinery needed to read the survey and draws the
bridge to financial modeling.

\textbf{Denoising diffusion and score-based foundations.}
DDPMs define a forward process that gradually corrupts data and train a neural
network to reverse that corruption~\cite{ho2020ddpm}. Score-based models learn
the score of perturbed data distributions and can be unified through reverse-time
SDEs~\cite{song2019ncsn,song2021sde}. In finance, this SDE view is more than
notation: it connects learned generative sampling to the stochastic-process
language already used for prices, rates, volatility, and risk-neutral valuation.

\textbf{Flow matching, rectified flow, and stochastic interpolants.}
Flow matching, rectified flow, and stochastic interpolants generalize the same
transport idea: learn a path from noise to data~\cite{lipman2023flow,
liu2023rectifiedflow,albergo2023interpolants}. They are increasingly relevant
because finance often needs conditional generation, fast sampling, and decision
loops where long reverse-diffusion chains are impractical.

\textbf{Conditioning and guidance.}
Conditioning is central in finance. Generators are rarely useful if they only
sample from an unconditional distribution; users often need scenarios conditioned
on regimes, assets, factors, stress levels, option surfaces, or observed market
states. Classifier and classifier-free guidance~\cite{dhariwal2021beatgan,
ho2022cfg} provide generic mechanisms, while finance-specific work adapts
conditioning to economic states, cross-sectional relations, and constraints.

\textbf{Sampling accelerators.}
Few-step sampling matters for trading, stress testing, and interactive risk
systems. DDIM~\cite{songddim2021}, improved diffusion design choices
~\cite{nichol2021improved,karras2022edm}, consistency models
~\cite{song2023consistency}, and flow-based samplers are therefore important
backbones even when the final paper is finance-facing rather than method-facing.

\textbf{Time-series and tabular backbones.}
General time-series diffusion models such as TimeGrad~\cite{rasul2021timegrad}
and CSDI~\cite{tashiro2021csdi}, together with tabular models such as
TabDDPM~\cite{kotelnikov2023tabddpm}, provide reusable architectures. Finance
papers typically modify these backbones for long memory, heavy tails,
cross-sectional dependence, missing data, mixed discrete-continuous fields, or
domain constraints.

\textbf{Diffusion for decision-making and control.}
Diffusion has also been used as a planner, policy, or trajectory model in
reinforcement learning~\cite{janner2022diffuser,ajay2022decisiondiffuser,
zhu2023diff4rl}. This matters for finance because portfolio construction,
execution, hedging, and market making are decision problems, not merely data
generation problems.

\section{Financial Time Series Data}\label{sec:fts}
Financial time series are the largest cluster in the current literature. The
modeled data include price paths, return series, volume, factors,
firm-characteristic panels, charts, macro variables, and portfolio returns.

\subsection{Unconditional Generation}\label{sec:fts-uncond}
Unconditional generation asks whether a model can synthesize financial series
whose marginal distributions, dependence, and stylized facts resemble real
markets. Early work generates synthetic asset paths~\cite{takahashi2024synthfts}
or uses diffusion as a market-dynamics simulator~\cite{lesniewski2024beyondmc}.
More recent work adds financial structure: GBM-inspired diffusion processes
~\cite{kim2025gbmdiff}, factor-structured return generation
~\cite{chen2025difffactor}, and quality-focused generators such as
Fiaingen~\cite{rozanec2025fiaingen}. The key evaluation question is not simply
sample realism, but whether generated paths preserve heavy tails, volatility
clustering, cross-sectional dependence, drawdowns, and downstream utility.

\subsection{Conditional Generation}\label{sec:fts-cond}
Conditional generation uses observed states, regimes, labels, or control signals
to steer the generated series. CoFinDiff conditions stock time-series generation
on user-specified controls~\cite{tanaka2025cofindiff}. Stress and inverse-problem
papers condition on crisis intensities, compressed observations, or optimization
needs~\cite{choudhary2025diffaugrl,guo2025compressedsensing,
zarifis2025scenariotree}. Conditional generation is especially important in
finance because users rarely want an average market: they want a plausible
market under a specified shock, regime, portfolio state, or observation mask.

\subsection{Prediction, Portfolio and Trading}\label{sec:fts-decision}
The same models are also used for forecasting, denoising, and decision support.
Diffusion-based forecasting and portfolio models target multivariate returns and
allocation objectives~\cite{cho2025diffolio}, while DiffSTOCK and DiffsFormer use
relational or factor-augmented conditioning for stock prediction
~\cite{daiya2024diffstock,gao2024diffsformer}. Denoising models refine noisy
financial signals before trading~\cite{wang2024ftsdenoiser}. Portfolio work
connects generative forecasts to allocation objectives, including mean-variance
and CVaR-aware optimization~\cite{gao2025factorcond,alzahrani2025marcd}.
Trading-oriented flow models such as FlowHFT treat the generator as part of a
policy-learning pipeline~\cite{li2025flowhft}.

\section{Limit Order Books Data}\label{sec:lob}
LOB data are high-frequency, event-driven, and constrained by market mechanics.
They may be represented as snapshots, volume/depth tensors, order-flow sequences,
event streams, or byte-level records.

\subsection{Generative Market Simulation}\label{sec:lob-sim}
LOB simulators aim to produce realistic market dynamics rather than isolated
price series. TRADES generates order flow for market simulation
~\cite{berti2025trades}; Painting the Market treats LOB states through
image-like representations~\cite{backhouse2025painting}; DiffVolume focuses on
volume generation in LOBs~\cite{wang2025diffvolume}; and byte-level or event
stream models move closer to raw exchange messages~\cite{li2025bytegen,
zheng2024lobdif}. These works must satisfy stronger tests than low-frequency
series: spread, depth, queue dynamics, order signs, price impact, and response to
trading flow all matter.

\subsection{Prediction}\label{sec:lob-pred}
LOB prediction and counterfactual generation ask what the book would look like
under an event, intervention, or future market state. DiffLOB targets
counterfactual generation of LOB snapshots~\cite{wang2026difflob}; diffusion
guided meta-agents generate controllable market behavior~\cite{huang2024digma};
and optimal-transport factorization studies market simulation through
microstructure regularities~\cite{hashimoto2025otpowerlaw}. The central
difficulty is responsiveness: generated books must react plausibly to actions
and shocks rather than merely match unconditional snapshot distributions.

\section{Tabular Data}\label{sec:tabular}
Financial tabular data include credit, payment, fund, fraud, customer, and
account-level records. These datasets are often mixed-type, imbalanced,
privacy-restricted, and constraint-heavy.

\subsection{Synthesis and Augmentation}\label{sec:tab-synth}
FinDiff introduced finance-specific tabular diffusion across credit, payment,
and fund data~\cite{sattarov2023findiff}. Later work extends this direction to
entity-based financial tables~\cite{liu2024enttabdiff}, class imbalance
~\cite{sattarov2024imbfindiff}, fraud generation~\cite{kuo2026emdt}, and
latent-space flow/diffusion augmentation~\cite{ihsan2025latentflow}. The common
goal is to create tables that improve downstream modeling or enable development
when original records cannot be shared.

\subsection{Privacy and Trustworthiness}\label{sec:tab-trust}
Tabular finance is where privacy and trust become central rather than optional.
Federated and differentially private tabular diffusion address the risk of
memorizing sensitive records~\cite{sattarov2024dpfedtabdiff,
sattarov2025dpfindiff}. Privacy-risk studies evaluate utility against
membership-inference and reconstruction threats~\cite{zuo2026privacyrisks}.
Trustworthy evaluation must therefore combine utility, privacy leakage,
constraint satisfaction, subgroup behavior, and robustness to class imbalance.

\section{Other Structured Financial Data}\label{sec:structured}
Not all financial objects are ordinary series, LOBs, or tables. Some are
structured surfaces, matrices, curves, or paths with strong mathematical
constraints.

\subsection{Volatility, Rates, and Dependence Objects}\label{sec:structured-objects}
Implied-volatility surfaces and interest-rate term structures are smooth,
arbitrage-sensitive objects indexed by maturity and strike or tenor. Diffusion
models have been used for IV-surface forecasting~\cite{jin2025ivsurface} and
term-structure generation~\cite{fukunishi2025termstructure}. Related structured
objects include correlation or covariance matrices, where positive
semidefiniteness and dependence realism are more important than pixel-like
fidelity.

\subsection{Derivative Paths, Pricing, and Hedging}\label{sec:structured-pricing}
Pricing and hedging applications move diffusion models toward risk-neutral
generation and stochastic control. Conditional diffusion has been proposed for
exotic option valuation~\cite{zhao2025exotic}; learned samplers have been used
for SDE solution paths~\cite{gao2025sdepaths}; and risk-neutral derivative
pricing work adapts generative diffusion to valuation under pricing measures
~\cite{tiwari2026riskneutral}. Treasury-futures synthesis similarly shows how
conditional time-series diffusion can target structured fixed-income instruments
~\cite{zhang2026tfcodit}.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Cross-cutting Methodological Trends}\label{sec:themes}
Across data domains, three patterns recur. First, conditioning is becoming the
default: regimes, factors, stress levels, observed masks, and market states are
what make generated samples useful. Second, finance papers increasingly build
structure into the generator or loss, from GBM- and factor-aware time-series
models to no-arbitrage and shape constraints in structured objects. Third,
diffusion is moving from offline synthesis toward decision pipelines, including
portfolio construction, robust reinforcement learning, and trading-aware
simulation.

\subsection{Evaluation and Benchmarking}\label{sec:eval}
Evaluation remains the field's binding constraint. Time-series papers report
stylized facts, distributional distances, and downstream forecasting or
portfolio metrics; LOB papers need microstructure realism and responsiveness;
tabular papers must balance utility and privacy; structured-object papers need
validity constraints such as positive semidefiniteness, smoothness, calibration,
and no-arbitrage. These metrics are rarely standardized across papers, so claims
of superiority are difficult to compare without shared datasets, chronological
splits, stress-period tests, and released code.

\subsection{Open Problems and Challenges}\label{sec:open}
Important open problems include tail and rare-event fidelity, non-stationarity,
economic constraints, computational efficiency, privacy auditing, and causal
counterfactuals. Finance also needs benchmarks that test out-of-regime
generalization rather than only in-distribution sample quality. For LOBs and
trading systems, the next step is interactive evaluation: models should be judged
by how generated markets respond to actions, not only by snapshot similarity.

\subsection{Discussion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Diffusion-family models are becoming a general-purpose tool for financial data
generation, inference, and decision support. Their promise comes from stable
training and flexible conditioning; their risk comes from evaluation gaps and
domain constraints that generic generative metrics do not capture. A data-first
taxonomy helps clarify what is actually being modeled and what validity checks
are required. Future progress will depend less on adding yet another sampler and
more on finance-specific benchmarks, transparent reporting, and models that
respect market structure.
